# 04 — Competitor Analysis

**Phase 1 · Version 0.1 · 2026-11-13**
**Method:** desk research on public websites, pricing pages, help centers, and third-party reviews. No vendor demos were run. Prices change; treat every number as indicative and re-verify before using it in a customer-facing document or a board deck.

---

## TL;DR

The market splits into five groups: deskless employee apps, enterprise communication platforms, WhatsApp Business API tools, safety/toolbox-talk platforms, and translation point solutions. Every serious employee app now offers some form of auto-translation. **Translation is table stakes; proof is not.**

The gap Understood can own:

1. **Zero install** — a link in the WhatsApp group the crew already uses, not an app account.
2. **Voice in, voice out** — the supervisor's voice note becomes translated text *and audio*; apps translate text inside their app.
3. **Instruction-level comprehension** — one question per instruction, per worker, with a first-attempt answer; apps offer read receipts, surveys, or training quizzes.
4. **A per-worker audit record** — exportable by instruction; apps export analytics, not a briefing record.
5. **A price for 100–1,000-worker GCC crews** — $1.40–$2.00 per active worker; per-seat apps cost 2–14× more at 300 workers.

**Strategic warning:** do not demo "translation" as the wow. Demo the worker's 40 seconds and the supervisor's record. Translation is the commodity; the workflow and the proof are the product.

## 4. Competitor profiles

### 4.1 Beekeeper (now part of LumApps)

**What it is:** a mobile-first frontline employee app — feed, chat, campaigns, surveys, hubs — acquired by LumApps in July 2025. The combined company reports 7M+ users and ~$150M revenue, backed by Bridgepoint, valued above $1B.

**Strengths:** strong frontline UX, broad language support (third-party sources describe 200+ languages for inline translation), mature admin and analytics, enterprise trust, deep integrations.

**Pricing:** no longer a standalone public pricing page; the former Beekeeper site routes to LumApps. Third-party listings in 2026 put plans around **$4–$9 per user/month**; treat as indicative.

**Why it doesn't fit the beachhead:**

- Requires an app install and account — the adoption killer for a 300-worker cleaning crew with mixed digital literacy.
- Translation is in-app text translation, not a supervisor voice note turned into per-language audio delivered by link.
- Comprehension is surveys/campaigns, not a per-instruction question tied to a delivery record.
- Enterprise sales motion and per-seat economics; a 300-worker FM site pays $1,200–$2,700/month before negotiation.
- Post-merger focus is the enterprise employee-experience platform, not GCC SME FM.

**What to learn:** the campaign/survey model proves employers want engagement data; our wedge is narrower and more defensible — the daily instruction and its proof.

---

### 4.2 Blink

**What it is:** a mobile-first frontline employee experience app — feed, chat, pages, surveys, learning — positioned around "the app frontline workers love to open every day."

**Strengths:** clean UX, strong translation coverage (publicly described as 30+ languages including Arabic), content authoring, engagement features, free trial.

**Pricing:** public page lists **Core $3.75/user/month** and **Pro $5.00/user/month**, billed annually; enterprise custom.

**Why it doesn't fit:**

- Install/login required; the worker must adopt another app.
- Translation is text/content translation inside the app; no supervisor voice-note → translated audio pipeline.
- No per-instruction comprehension record or audit export for a briefing.
- At 300 workers, $1,125–$1,500/month before discounts — 2–3× our price for a different job.

**What to learn:** Blink's content and adoption playbook is a good reference for supervisor UX; our worker flow must be simpler than an app.

---

### 4.3 Connecteam

**What it is:** an all-in-one deskless workforce platform sold as three hubs — Operations (scheduling, tasks, time clock), Communications (updates, chat, surveys), HR — with a free plan for up to 10 users.

**Strengths:** strong SME fit, broad feature set, free entry tier, automatic translation of updates, mobile app, aggressive marketing.

**Pricing:** third-party summaries list **$29/month for up to 30 users (Basic), $49 (Advanced), $99 (Expert) per hub**, with per-user pricing beyond the included band. A 300-worker deployment across hubs is materially more than the entry number.

**Why it doesn't fit:**

- App install and account; the worker experience is a workforce app, not a 90-second link.
- Auto-translation applies to updates and chat; it does not turn a voice note into per-language audio with a comprehension check.
- No per-worker briefing audit; analytics are engagement/ops metrics.
- Three hubs create configuration and cost that a 300-worker FM site does not need for this job.

**What to learn:** Connecteam proves SMEs will pay for deskless tools; our pitch must be narrower ("the 9-minute briefing proof"), not "all-in-one."

---

### 4.4 Staffbase

**What it is:** an enterprise employee communication platform — intranet, employee app, email/SMS channels, campaigns, analytics — focused on large, distributed workforces.

**Strengths:** enterprise-grade governance, multilingual content (third-party sources describe 110+ languages), integrations, strong for corporate communications.

**Pricing:** custom annual quote. One competitor review reports a **£25k–£45k/year (~$34k–$61k)** range for an app + intranet package; this is a third-party estimate, not a quote.

**Why it doesn't fit:** enterprise sales cycle, app install, corporate-comms orientation, price far above an SME FM site, and no instruction-level comprehension/audit workflow.

**What to learn:** enterprise buyers pay for governance and proof; our trust page and audit export should borrow that seriousness without the enterprise weight.

---

### 4.5 Mitti (formerly SafetyCulture)

**What it is:** a workplace operations/safety platform built around inspections, checklists, training, and toolbox talks. SafetyCulture rebranded to **Mitti on 11 August 2026**, betting on AI and insurance-adjacent expansion.

**Strengths:** massive SMB safety footprint, mobile app, inspection and training workflows, multi-language templates, strong brand in safety.

**Pricing:** public page lists **Free (up to 10 users), Premium $24/seat/month, Enterprise**; training/onboarding included in paid tiers. At 300 seats that is ~$7,200/month.

**Why it doesn't fit:**

- Built for inspections and training content, not a supervisor's ad hoc daily voice note.
- App-based; workers need an account.
- Multi-language templates are manual/ISO-coded, not voice-note → per-language audio.
- Safety positioning carries certification expectations we deliberately avoid.

**What to learn:** Mitti validates that safety-adjacent buyers pay for records; our positioning must stay clearly "communication support, not training/certification."

---

### 4.6 Wati (WhatsApp Business API / BSP)

**What it is:** a WhatsApp Business API platform — shared inbox, templates, broadcasts, automation — used by SMBs to message customers and teams.

**Strengths:** WhatsApp-native delivery, template approvals, automation, integrations.

**Pricing:** **Growth $59/month (3 users), Pro $119 (5), Business $279 (5)**, annual billing for listed prices, plus WhatsApp/Meta template and conversation fees. From 1 October 2026, service messages and utility templates inside the 24-hour window are billable per message.

**Why it doesn't fit:**

- It is a delivery layer, not an instruction/comprehension product: no per-worker language page, no audio generation, no question, no audit record.
- Requires Meta business verification, template approval, and ongoing message costs.
- Building the missing layer on top of Wati is possible — but that is our product, and v0.1 deliberately avoids API costs by sharing links in the existing group.

**What to learn:** WhatsApp delivery is a v0.2 add-on, not the wedge. The link-first MVP is cheaper and faster; Wati's 2026 pricing changes reinforce that.

---

### 4.7 Relay TeamTranslate

**What it is:** a rugged push-to-talk radio/service with real-time speech-to-speech translation for frontline teams (30+ languages), including a TeamTranslate feature.

**Strengths:** live conversation translation, durable hardware, works in noisy/offline-ish environments, strong for real-time coordination.

**Pricing:** quote-based; hardware plus subscription.

**Why it doesn't fit:** it solves live conversation, not a scheduled instruction with a per-worker record. It requires hardware per team, has no comprehension question or audit export, and does not ride the existing WhatsApp group.

**What to learn:** voice-first is a real workflow; but live translation and asynchronous instruction proof are different jobs. Do not blur the pitch.

---

### 4.8 Tyfoom

**What it is:** an employee engagement/training platform built around short video microlearning, communication, and quizzes; often used for safety training.

**Strengths:** training content, quizzes, engagement, mobile app, "viewed/understood" language.

**Pricing:** quote-based; the app is free to download but requires an employer subscription.

**Why it doesn't fit:** training content and assigned lessons, not a supervisor's daily voice note; app install; no WhatsApp-link workflow; content authoring burden.

**What to learn:** the word "understood" is contested; our definition (first-attempt correct + Done within 24h) must be explicit and defensible.

---

### 4.9 modONE

**What it is:** a multilingual safety training app for diverse crews, claiming no downloads required; focused on safety training and communication.

**Strengths:** multilingual safety content, low-friction access claim, safety use case.

**Pricing:** quote-based.

**Why it doesn't fit:** training content, not ad hoc instructions; safety certification positioning; no per-instruction comprehension/audit workflow for a supervisor's briefing.

**What to learn:** "no downloads" is a strong message; verify their implementation and make sure our zero-install claim is technically precise (PWA/link, no app store).

---

### 4.10 Shavely and translation chat tools

**What it is:** team chat that translates messages between languages (Shavely claims 29 languages; other WhatsApp translator apps exist).

**Strengths:** cheap, simple, multilingual chat.

**Why it doesn't fit:** chat is not an instruction workflow; no audio generation, no comprehension question, no delivery board, no audit export. Workers still need the app or a translated chat.

**What to learn:** translation alone is a feature, not a product. The buyer pays for the outcome and the record.

---

### 4.11 The real competitor: WhatsApp + verbal + paper

**What it is:** the status quo. A supervisor sends a voice note to the group, briefs verbally at the start of the shift, and assumes understanding. Audit evidence is a paper sign-in sheet or a phone photo.

**Strengths:** free, familiar, zero training, works on every phone, no procurement.

**Weaknesses:** no per-worker language guarantee, no comprehension check, no record, buried within hours, relies on one bilingual colleague, no follow-up for the worker who didn't understand.

**Why it wins today:** habit and zero cost. **Why it loses:** a single incident or audit finding costs more than a year of Understood.

**What to learn:** the product must fit inside the WhatsApp habit, not replace it. Every extra step for the supervisor is a reason to revert.

## 5. What the market tells us

### 5.1 Translation is table stakes

Beekeeper, Blink, Connecteam, Staffbase, and others now advertise auto-translation or multilingual content. A pitch that leads with "we translate instructions" is not differentiated. Lead with:

- **No install** — the worker opens a link; no app, no account.
- **Voice in, audio out** — the supervisor keeps the voice-note habit; the worker hears their language.
- **Proof** — one question per instruction, per worker, with a first-attempt answer and an export.

### 5.2 The enterprise end is consolidating

LumApps + Beekeeper (July 2025) created a $1B+ employee-experience platform with 7M users; SafetyCulture became Mitti (August 2026) and is expanding into AI/insurance. Consolidation validates the category but pushes the incumbents upmarket. A 300-worker Dubai cleaning company is not their priority. That is the opening.

**Implication:** move fast, own the GCC FM workflow, and lock design partners before an incumbent launches a "frontline lite" WhatsApp-link product.

### 5.3 Per-seat pricing punishes the beachhead

At 300 workers:

| Solution | Indicative monthly | Multiple of Understood ($510) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Understood | $510 | 1× |
| Blink | $1,125–$1,500 | 2.2–2.9× |
| Beekeeper | $1,200–$2,700 | 2.4–5.3× |
| Connecteam (one hub, entry) | ~$290+ | 0.6×+ (but app + limited scope) |
| Mitti | $7,200 | 14× |
| Staffbase (org estimate) | ~$2,800–$5,100 | 5.5–10× |
| WhatsApp + manual | $0 + supervisor time | — |

The pricing story is not "cheaper than apps" alone; it is "cheaper than apps **and** it does the job they don't." Connecteam can be cheaper at the entry tier, but it is an app-based ops hub, not a proof workflow.

### 5.4 WhatsApp API costs are rising

Wati's October 2026 note that service messages and utility templates inside the 24-hour window are billable per message reinforces the v0.1 decision: **share links in the existing group, no API costs, no template approval.** Add API delivery later when the customer asks and the volume justifies it.

### 5.5 Install is the adoption killer

Every employee app requires an install and an account. For a worker on a personal low-end Android, with limited storage and data, that is a real barrier — and for a supervisor, it is another system to explain. The zero-install link is not a gimmick; it is the distribution strategy.

### 5.6 Safety platforms own training, not daily instructions

Mitti and modONE are strong at inspections and training content. They are not built for a supervisor's 6:40 a.m. voice note that changes daily. Understood should integrate with that world (export, disclaimer) rather than compete with it.

## 6. Positioning and battlecards

### 6.1 Positioning statement

*For GCC operations and HSE managers with multilingual frontline crews, Understood is the voice-first communication system that delivers every instruction in each worker's own language and proves it was understood — without an app, a login, or a new channel. Unlike employee apps, it does not ask workers to install anything; unlike WhatsApp alone, it produces a per-worker record.*

### 6.2 Battlecards (internal)

**vs. Beekeeper / LumApps**

- They are an enterprise employee-experience platform; we are a focused proof workflow.
- They require an app; we require a link.
- They translate text in-app; we turn a voice note into per-language audio.
- They show engagement analytics; we show who understood the instruction.
- Talk track: "Keep your app for company news. Run us on one crew for the daily briefing and compare the record."

**vs. Blink**

- Similar app-first model, strong content/engagement, 30+ languages.
- Our advantage: zero install, voice-note input, audio output, per-instruction comprehension, price.
- Talk track: "Blink is where workers read company content. Understood is how you know the 6:40 briefing landed."

**vs. Connecteam**

- Strong SME ops suite (scheduling, tasks, chat) at a low entry price.
- Our advantage: no app, instruction-specific proof, per-active-worker pricing; not another hub to configure.
- Talk track: "Use Connecteam for scheduling. Use us for the instruction that changes every morning."

**vs. Mitti (SafetyCulture)**

- Inspections and training records, not ad hoc instructions; app-based; safety certification expectations.
- Our advantage: daily voice note, six worker languages, link-based, communication record with a disclaimer.
- Talk track: "Mitti proves the inspection happened. Understood proves the worker understood what to do about it."

**vs. Wati / WhatsApp API**

- A delivery layer; no translation/audio/comprehension/audit; message fees and template approvals.
- Our advantage: link-first, zero messaging cost, complete workflow today; API delivery is a later add-on.
- Talk track: "Wati sends the message. Understood makes sure it was understood."

**vs. Relay**

- Live push-to-talk translation on hardware; different job.
- Our advantage: asynchronous instruction, per-worker record, no hardware, works on the phones workers already have.
- Talk track: "Relay is for the conversation in the shaft. Understood is for the briefing before the shift."

**vs. Tyfoom / modONE**

- Training content and microlearning; app-based; content authoring burden.
- Our advantage: supervisor's own voice, daily instructions, comprehension check, link-based.
- Talk track: "Training is monthly. Instructions are daily. We handle the daily."

**vs. doing nothing (WhatsApp + verbal + paper)**

- Free and familiar, but no proof, no per-worker language guarantee, no follow-up.
- Our advantage: fits the existing habit, adds the record.
- Talk track: "You already send the voice note. We make it land — and give you the receipt."

## 8. Implications for Phase 1 and the plan

### 8.1 Interview questions to add

- "Which apps do your workers already have installed? Which ones did they stop using?"
- "Have you tried translating instructions? What happened?"
- "What did you do before WhatsApp? What would make you switch back?"
- "If an app required installation, would your crew do it? Who wouldn't?"

### 8.2 Messaging changes

- Do not lead with "AI translation." Lead with **"the 90-second proof, no app."**
- In the demo, show the worker page and the record before showing the translation preview.
- Use the phrase **"record of comprehension"**, not "compliance" or "training."

### 8.3 Pricing implications

- Keep $1.40–$2.00 per active worker and the $149 minimum; the app comparison supports it.
- Do not price per seat; it would put us in the app category and punish the beachhead.
- The pilot fee ($490) is low enough to approve and high enough to qualify; the competitor teardown supports it.

### 8.4 Product implications

- Zero-install is non-negotiable for v0.1.
- Voice-note input + audio output is the differentiator; protect it in scope.
- Per-instruction comprehension + export is the moat; do not bury it as an analytics feature.
- Arabic supervisor UI and six worker languages are parity-plus, not extras.
- Seats/WhatsApp API remain v0.2; do not sell them early.
