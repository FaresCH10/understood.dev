# 02 — Interview Kit

**Phase 1 · Version 0.1 · 2026-11-13**
**Purpose:** 10 research conversations that test the Phase 1 hypotheses in [01-validation-plan.md](./01-validation-plan.md).
**Rule:** research, not sales. No pitch until the final five minutes. No invented notes.

---

## 1. Who to talk to

| Segment | Target | Why | Where to find them |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Operations / facilities managers | 6 | Own the daily briefing and supervisor behavior | LinkedIn, referrals, FM communities |
| HSE / QHSE / safety managers | 2 | Own the audit answer and risk narrative | LinkedIn, HSE groups, consultant intros |
| Owners / GMs | 2 | Own the budget and the buying decision | Referrals, company pages, events |

**Company profile for interviews:** UAE FM, cleaning, or integrated facilities; 50–1,000 workers; at least three languages in the roster. Pilot candidates must be 100–1,000 workers.

**Sources (max four interviews from one source):**

1. Lebanese diaspora and university alumni in UAE ops/HSE roles (AUB, LAU, USJ, NDU, Balamand).
2. LinkedIn outbound with the search strings below.
3. UAE FM/cleaning/HSE WhatsApp and Facebook communities — participate first, no links.
4. Referrals from every interviewee (ask for two names).
5. One industry meetup or association event if timing aligns (MEFMA, Dubai Chamber).

**Do not interview:** vendors selling to FM (they have a pitch), single-language crews, construction-only HSE in this phase, or friends who will only be polite. If the first three are friendly, recruit two known skeptics.

## 3. Screener (send before booking)

> Hi {name} — I'm researching how UAE facilities and cleaning teams brief crews that speak several languages. Not selling anything. Would you have 25 minutes in the next two weeks for a short conversation about how instructions reach workers today?
>
> Quick context so I don't waste your time: roughly how many workers are on your sites, and which languages do they speak?

**Qualify if:**

- They run or oversee crews (not a vendor).
- 50+ workers, at least two languages (three preferred).
- They can describe the current instruction flow.
- They agree to 25 minutes.

**Disqualify politely if:** single language, no crew responsibility, or they only want a sales demo. Offer to stay in touch and move on.

## 4. The 30-minute script

### 4.1 Opening (2 min)

> Thanks for the time. I'm researching how multilingual frontline crews receive instructions in the Gulf — how work gets communicated, and how you know it landed. This is not a sales call. I'll take notes; I won't record unless you're comfortable. I'll anonymize your company in anything I write. You can skip any question. Is that okay?

Ask: role, how long in the role, site type, roughly how many workers and crews, which languages.

### 4.2 Current flow (8 min)

Ask for a specific recent instruction, not a general description.

1. "Walk me through yesterday's or this morning's instruction. What was it?"
2. "Who sent it, and how — voice note, text, face-to-face, app?"
3. "What language did you send it in? What language did the workers receive?"
4. "What happens after you send it? How do you know it was received?"
5. "How do you know it was understood?"
6. "If someone doesn't understand, what do they do? Who do they ask?"
7. "How does a new worker learn the job today?"
8. "Can you show me the WhatsApp group or the last voice note? Redact names if you like."

**Probes:** "Tell me more." "What happened next?" "Who else was involved?" "How long did that take?"

### 4.3 The incident (5 min)

9. "Tell me about the last time an instruction was misunderstood. What happened?"
10. "What did it cost — time, rework, a client complaint, a safety issue?"
11. "What changed after that, if anything?"
12. "Has it happened more than once? What's the pattern?"

If they cannot recall an incident, ask about a near miss or a repeated instruction. A vague answer is evidence too.

### 4.4 Audit and proof (4 min)

13. "Has a client or an HSE auditor ever asked you to prove workers were briefed?"
14. "What did you show them? Can you show me what that record looks like?"
15. "If you had to produce a per-worker record today, how long would it take?"
16. "What would make that record more credible?"

Ask to see the artifact. A photo of a paper sign-off sheet or a WhatsApp screenshot is stronger than an opinion.

### 4.5 Language and devices (4 min)

17. "Which languages are on the roster, roughly how many workers each?"
18. "Who translates today? What happens when that person is off-site?"
19. "Do workers read English? Do they read their own script comfortably? Do they prefer audio?"
20. "What phones do workers use — personal Android, iPhone, shared site phone? Data?"
21. "Do workers use WhatsApp voice notes themselves?"

### 4.6 Reaction to the concept (5 min — only now)

Describe the workflow neutrally, in one breath:

> Imagine the supervisor records a 30-second voice note. It becomes text and audio in each worker's language. The worker opens a link in the WhatsApp group, hears it, answers one question, and taps Done. The supervisor sees who understood, who needs help, and who never opened it.

22. "What's your first reaction?"
23. "What would make you trust it? What would make you not trust it?"
24. "What would stop you from using it?"
25. "Who would need to approve it?"
26. "If it worked, what would you do differently?"

**Do not ask:** "Do you like it?" "Would you use an app?" "How much would you pay?" (price comes after the value discussion, and only as a reaction).

### 4.7 Price reaction (2 min — optional, only if the conversation is warm)

> The pilot is $490 for eight weeks, one site, up to 150 workers, and the fee is credited to the first invoice. After that it's per active worker — roughly $150 to $630 a month for a site like yours.

27. "What's your reaction to that?"
28. "Who else would need to see it before you'd pay for a pilot?"

Do not defend the price. Note the reaction, the comparison they make, and the approval path.

### 4.8 Close (2 min)

29. "Who else should I talk to who runs multilingual crews?" (Ask for two names.)
30. "Would you be open to a 20-minute demo with one crew, no commitment?" (Only if they fit.)
31. "Can I follow up in two weeks with what I learned?"

Thank them. Send the one-page communication audit within 24 hours if they opted in. Do not add them to a marketing list.

## 5. Consent and privacy

Read or adapt:

> This is research, not a sales call. I'll take notes and anonymize your company. I won't record audio unless you say yes. You can skip any question or stop at any time. If you share a roster or screenshot, please redact worker names and phone numbers; I'll store it privately and delete raw material after 30 days.

- Default: notes only. If recording, ask explicitly and record the permission.
- Anonymize companies as Company A/B/C in notes; keep the mapping in one private file.
- Never store worker names, phone numbers, or ID documents.
- Do not quote an individual by name without written permission.
- Delete raw audio after 30 days; keep anonymized notes until the pilot gate.

## 7. Synthesis method

### 7.1 After five interviews (27 Nov–3 Dec)

1. **Count the gates:** pain, channel, audit, languages, payment. Use the tracker, not memory.
2. **Affinity map:** group quotes under four themes — current flow, incidents, audit, objections. Add a fifth: "things we did not expect."
3. **Segment differences:** ops vs HSE vs owner. If they disagree, write both views; do not average.
4. **Disconfirming evidence:** list every answer that contradicts the plan. This is the most important page.
5. **Objection ranking:** frequency × severity. The top three become content and sales answers.
6. **Implications:** one line per hypothesis — confirmed, refuted, unclear, and what to change.

### 7.2 After ten interviews (4–10 Dec)

Produce a two-page synthesis:

- Gate scorecard (from [01 §7](./01-validation-plan.md#7-gate-scorecard)).
- Five strongest quotes (verbatim, anonymized).
- Top three objections and the response.
- What surprised us.
- What we will change in the PRD/backlog/pricing/channel.
- What we will not change, and why.

### 7.3 Quote bank

Keep exact language. "We assume they understood" is stronger than "there is a comprehension gap." Use the quote bank for the landing page, outreach, and pilot proposal — with permission and anonymization.

### 7.4 What not to do

- Do not average qualitative answers into a fake score.
- Do not count the same company twice.
- Do not discard negative evidence because the interviewee was friendly.
- Do not extrapolate 10 interviews into a TAM.
- Do not let one enthusiastic owner override six supervisors.

## 9. What good evidence sounds like

| Weak (opinion) | Strong (evidence) |
|---|---|
| "Yeah, communication is a problem." | "Last Tuesday the night shift cleaned the wrong floor because the voice note was only in Hindi; we redid 40 rooms." |
| "Workers prefer WhatsApp." | "Here is the group; the supervisor sends a voice note at 6:40 every day; 32 workers are in it." |
| "We have no audit record." | "The client asked for briefing records; we sent a photo of a sign-in sheet." |
| "Translation would help." | "Our Malayalam speakers wait for the one Malayalam-speaking supervisor; when he is off, they ask a colleague." |
| "I'd probably pay for that." | "Who invoices you? We pay monthly by transfer; send the pilot proposal to me and I'll sign it." |
