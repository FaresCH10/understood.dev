## TL;DR

This is not a marketing launch. It is a **12-month, founder-led sales system** with one beachhead, one offer, and a binary gate. The product exists to produce one artifact: a paid customer who can show that their workers understood.

> A pilot is not a favor to a friendly customer. It is the first paid proof that the category exists.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who do we sell to first? | UAE facilities management & cleaning companies, 100–1,000 workers, 3+ languages in the roster, crews already run on WhatsApp |
| What do we sell? | An 8-week paid pilot — **$490**, one site, ≤150 workers, fully credited to the first invoice — then **$1.40–2.00 per active worker/month** |

**The one-sentence strategy:** sell the pilot to one type of company in one city, make the worker experience undeniable, and convert evidence into a reference — repeat ten times before spending a dollar on acquisition.

## 1. The launch thesis

### 1.1 What we are actually selling

We are not selling an "employee communication platform." That category is crowded, English-first, install-gated, and priced per seat. We are selling a **record of comprehension**: the supervisor's instruction, in the worker's language, with evidence that the worker understood it.

That distinction drives every launch decision:

| If we sold… | We would need… | Instead, we… |
|---|---|---|
| An employee app | App-store presence, IT approval, worker installs | Sell a link in the WhatsApp group the crew already uses |
| A translation tool | Trust in machine translation alone | Make preview and supervisor confirmation mandatory |
| A safety training product | Certification, curriculum, liability | Sell communication support, with a disclaimer on every worker page |
| A dashboard | Data integration, admin buy-in | Sell the supervisor's daily ritual: send, see, chase, prove |

### 1.2 Five launch principles

1. **Sell the pilot, not the platform.** A time-boxed, paid, evidence-generating engagement converts skepticism into data. The platform story comes after the numbers.
2. **One ICP, one offer, one price.** Every exception before 10 customers is a distraction disguised as revenue.
3. **The founder is the channel.** At $150–$630/month and this geography, trust moves person-to-person. There is no SDR, no agency, no ads.
4. **The worker experience is the demo.** If a Malayalam-speaking worker can't finish in 90 seconds on a low-end phone, nothing else in the sales deck matters.
5. **Decide the stop rules before the emotion.** The gates in §17 are written now so a disappointing month produces a decision, not a discount.

### 1.3 What this launch is not

- **Not a Product Hunt launch.** The buyer is an FM operations manager in Dubai, not a product enthusiast. A PH spike produces signups we cannot serve and zero ICP conversations.
- **Not a paid-acquisition launch.** At $300 average MRR and a 3–8 week sales cycle, CAC payback on ads is negative before the first renewal.
- **Not an enterprise launch.** Companies above 1,000 workers bring procurement, security review, and multi-site requirements that v0.1 deliberately does not serve (single owner login, no seats).
- **Not a multi-country launch.** UAE first, KSA only after five UAE references and a partner who can invoice locally.
- **Not a feature race.** During the pilot, only P0 fixes ship. Requests go to the v0.2 backlog with a date, not a promise.

### 1.4 Why now, and why this founder

The window is real but not infinite: deskless-app vendors are moving down-market, WhatsApp Business API costs are falling, and GCC clients increasingly ask FM companies for proof of communicated instructions. A solo founder in Beirut can sell remotely into Dubai — only a two-hour time difference, a link-based product, no relocation — and can run a white-glove pilot that a larger vendor cannot afford to.

The constraint is also the strategy: with no network and no budget, we cannot win on reach. We can win on **depth per pilot** and on a product that makes the worker's 90 seconds feel effortless.

## 3. Positioning, narrative, and proof

### 3.1 Positioning statement

*For GCC operations and HSE managers with multilingual frontline crews, Understood is the voice-first communication system that delivers every instruction in each worker's own language and proves it was understood — unlike employee apps that require installation, English menus, and read receipts that prove nothing.*

### 3.2 The three-sentence pitch

> Your supervisor already sends voice notes to the crew's WhatsApp group. We turn that voice note into every worker's language — text and audio — and add one comprehension question. By the end of the shift you can show who understood the instruction, who needs help, and who never opened it.

### 3.3 Tagline and Arabic descriptor

- **English:** *Say it once. Know it landed.*
- **Arabic (pending native review):** «قُلها مرة. واعرف أنها وصلت.»
- **Product descriptor:** «مفهوم» — *understood* — used as the Arabic campaign word, never as a transliteration of the English name.

All Arabic customer-facing copy is written or reviewed by a native speaker before it ships (PRD §12). No machine-translated slogans.

### 3.4 Message hierarchy by persona

| Persona | Lead message | Supporting proof | Do not say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner / GM | "You cannot manage what your crew didn't understand." | Understood rate, rework avoided, audit export | "AI-powered platform" |
| HSE lead | "A timestamped record of what was communicated — in the worker's language." | CSV with first-attempt answers; preview step | "Compliant", "certified", "OSHA" |
| Supervisor | "Say it once. See who got it. Stop chasing." | 90-second worker flow; board; help requests | "Change how you work" |
| Worker | "Your language. One tap. 90 seconds." | Audio-first page; no install; no login | Anything about monitoring |

### 3.5 The proof stack

Every sales conversation uses the same four proofs, in this order:

1. **The live demo on their phone** — their language mix, a real 20-second voice note, a real worker page (§Appendix B).
2. **The numbers from the pilot** — response, understood, help, time-to-share; a one-page report, not a dashboard screenshot.
3. **The audit artifact** — a CSV opened in Excel, with Arabic-safe encoding and timestamps.
4. **The trust page** — what data we hold, where it lives, who processes it, how to delete it.

No slide deck before proof. If the demo fails on their phone, the meeting is over — and that is the correct outcome.

### 3.6 Claims policy (non-negotiable)

- Never say **compliant, certified, OSHA-approved, safety training, guarantees understanding**.
- Say **communication record, comprehension check, supports your procedures, audit export**.
- The worker footer disclaimer (WORK-10) is always on and translated into all six languages.
- Any statistic in content must come from our own pilot data (with permission), a cited public source, or be labeled as an estimate.
- No customer logo, quote, or metric without written consent. Anonymized ("a Dubai FM company with 220 workers") until consent is signed.

### 3.7 The name decision

"Understood" is still a working title. It collides with a well-known US nonprofit (Understood.org) and may be weak as a trademark in the UAE.

**Decision deadline: 4 Dec 2026.** Before the pilot agreement is signed:

- Run a UAE trademark check in classes 9, 35, and 42 (budget: use a free registry search first; pay for counsel only if ambiguous).
- Secure the domain and social handles. Buy the domain regardless of the final name; it is required for Resend deliverability and trust (TDD §15).
- If "Understood" is clean for our category, keep it and use «مفهوم» as the Arabic descriptor.
- If not, launch customer-facing as **Mafhoom / «مفهوم»** and keep "Understood" as the internal product name. The docs do not need to change; the brand does.

Do not print merchandise, pay for a logo, or sign a lease on a name before this check.

### 4.3 What is included

- Unlimited crews and instructions within fair use.
- All six worker languages; all audience languages generated (D6).
- Audio + text variants; one comprehension question per instruction.
- Live board, help flow, CSV audit export (instruction and org-wide).
- One owner login per organization (v0.1; additional seats are v0.2).
- Email magic-link support during business hours (Beirut time, Mon–Fri).
- Nightly backups, best-effort availability ≥99%, security updates.
- **Fair use: 150 TTS minutes per org per month.** Cached audio is free. The in-product meter warns at 150 minutes (SET-3). Beyond 150, overage is **$0.35/minute**, invoiced in arrears and never interrupting service. At 12 instructions/month × 4 languages × 30 seconds, a normal supervisor uses ~24 minutes.

### 4.4 What is not included (say it early)

- WhatsApp Business API delivery, SMS, push notifications (v0.2).
- Multiple supervisor seats, SSO, role permissions (v0.2).
- Custom feature development, bespoke integrations, on-premise or in-country hosting.
- Certified training content, safety curriculum, or compliance guarantees.
- A 24/7 support line; enterprise SLA; uptime credits.
- Bulk-worker imports above 1,000 per site without a scoped plan.

Stating exclusions in the first call prevents the pilot from turning into a procurement negotiation for a product that does not exist yet.

### 4.5 Pricing logic and ROI framing

The price is anchored to per-worker cost ($1.40–2.00), not per-seat software. For a 220-worker site at $374/month:

- One avoided rework incident per month (illustrative cost: AED 1,500–3,000) covers the subscription 1.5–3×.
- Thirty minutes/day of supervisor chasing, at an illustrative AED 30/hour, is ~AED 975/month — 2.5× the subscription.
- A single audit finding or client complaint is usually more expensive than a year of the product.

Label these as **illustrative**, not measured. The pilot's end report replaces them with the customer's own numbers: baseline chase time, instructions sent, response rate, understood rate.

### 4.6 Payment and billing reality

- **Pilot:** prepaid before kickoff. Invoice via Payoneer (bank transfer or card), USD.
- **Paid plan:** first contract quarterly prepaid based on the current roster; then monthly in arrears on active workers after three on-time payments.
- **No Stripe, no Tap, no PayTabs** — not available to a Lebanon-registered merchant (Product Brief §7).
- **Merchant of record** (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy — confirm current Lebanon support) when automated subscriptions ship in v0.2. Do not build billing in v0.1; invoice manually.
- **UAE entity** only when MRR is ~$1.5–4k and a customer requires a local vendor. Until then, a Lebanese sole proprietorship with a clear invoice template is sufficient for SMEs.
- **VAT:** prices exclude VAT. Do not collect VAT until an accountant confirms the treatment for cross-border B2B services; state "VAT exclusive" on every invoice.
- **Terms:** 14 days for pilot and monthly invoices; 30 days only for established customers. The first contract is prepaid — this filters serious buyers and protects cash.

## 5. The pilot

### 5.1 Why the pilot is paid

A free pilot optimizes for politeness. A paid pilot optimizes for use. The $490 does three jobs:

1. **Commitment filter** — the champion must spend social capital to get it approved.
2. **Cost coverage** — it pays for the domain, the first months of infrastructure, and the Dubai trip.
3. **Evidence contract** — both sides agree on success criteria before the first instruction, so the conversion conversation is about numbers, not impressions.

### 5.2 Pilot profile

One site. One owner login. One champion. Up to 150 workers. At least three languages. Eight weeks. A named economic buyer who will attend kickoff and the value review. A supervisor who sends instructions at least three times a week today.

If the company has 300 workers, pilot one site or one crew cluster of ≤150. Expansion is the conversion conversation, not the pilot scope.

### 5.3 Pilot agreement (outline in Appendix A)

The agreement is two pages, not a master services contract. It contains: scope, fee and credit, dates, success criteria, obligations, data terms (DPA-lite), confidentiality, IP, termination and refund, case-study consent (optional), and the "not a safety certification" limitation. It is signed before kickoff; the invoice is paid before the first instruction is shared.

### 5.4 Obligations

**Understood (founder):**

- Import the roster, set up the org, and run a 20-minute kickoff.
- Create the first instruction live with the supervisor and test the worker page on two real phones.
- Provide WhatsApp forward copy in English and Arabic (PRD Appendix).
- Join a 20-minute weekly review; send a one-page scorecard within 24 hours.
- Respond to support messages within one business day; same-day for blockers during weeks 1–2.
- Ship only P0 fixes during the pilot; log every request with a date.
- Deliver an end-of-pilot report: baseline vs. pilot metrics, quotes, and a recommendation.

**Customer:**

- Name a champion and an economic buyer; attend kickoff and the value review.
- Forward the link to the crew within 30 minutes of sharing, with the provided message.
- Send at least three instructions per week for the first four weeks.
- Review the weekly scorecard and give honest feedback, including "this isn't working."
- Provide a roster CSV with names, languages, and (optionally) phone numbers — phone can be blank.
- Tell us within 24 hours if a worker reports a privacy or safety concern.

### 5.5 Week-by-week

| Week | Focus | Founder actions | Evidence produced |
|---|---|---|---|
| **−2 to 0** | Contract and setup | Agreement, DPA, invoice, roster import, kickoff, baseline interview (instructions/week, languages, chase time) | Signed agreement, baseline document, org ready |
| **1** | First loop | Three instructions: voice, text, photo. Daily check-in. Test on champion's and one worker's phone | First share, first worker completion, time-to-share |
| **2** | Fix the friction | Diagnose <50% response: link placement, name clarity, language mix, audio playback. Add a second crew if response ≥60% | Weekly scorecard, fix log |
| **3** | Habit check | Supervisor is sending ≥3/week without reminders? Introduce the audit export | Instructions/week, export used |
| **4** | Mid-pilot review | Buyer + champion: numbers vs. baseline; continue, adjust, or stop. Ask for two referral names | Decision memo, referral pipeline |
| **5** | Proof collection | Case-study data (consent), worker quotes (optional, no PII), Task vs. Info mix | Draft case study, metrics pack |
| **6** | Value review prep | Calculate response, understood, help, chase time saved; draft one-page proposal | Proposal draft |
| **7** | Value review | 30 minutes: numbers, cost of status quo, three options, sign or schedule | Signed contract or documented no |
| **8** | Transition | Case-study interview, referral ask, end-of-pilot report, support handover | Report, case study consent, referrals |

### 5.6 Success criteria (written into the agreement)

The pilot is **successful** when, over the final four consecutive weeks:

- Response rate ≥70% (opened ÷ targeted, 24h).
- Understood rate ≥60% (first-attempt correct + Done within 24h ÷ targeted).
- Supervisor sends ≥3 instructions/week in at least four of the last five weeks.
- Help rate between 5% and 15% (0% is a warning, not a win).
- At least one audit export used by the buyer or HSE lead.
- Buyer and champion both say they would recommend it (≥8/10).

**The pilot gate to paid** (PRD §2): a design partner converts **and** keeps ≥70% response for four consecutive weeks. A pilot that hits the metrics but does not convert is a sales failure, not a product failure — diagnose separately.

### 5.7 Stop, refund, and extension rules

- **Before the first share:** full refund, no questions.
- **After the first share, customer stops:** no refund; 100% of the fee credits a paid plan if they sign within 90 days.
- **Product failure:** if Understood stops the pilot because the product cannot deliver the scope, unused weeks are refunded pro-rata.
- **Early success:** if the customer wants to convert in week 5, convert; do not force eight weeks for the ceremony.
- **Extension:** one two-week extension at $150, only if a specific blocker (a holiday week, a roster change) prevented a fair test. Extensions are not discounts.

**Kill criteria for the pilot itself:** if response is below 40% at the end of week 2 after one documented fix, or below 50% at the end of week 3, stop and write the post-mortem. Do not extend a failing pilot to save the relationship.

### 5.8 What every pilot produces, win or lose

- A baseline and an outcome, measured the same way.
- A list of the three moments where the supervisor hesitated.
- A list of worker-language issues that only real workers reveal.
- At least one referral name, if the champion is honest.
- A testimonial or a documented objection — both are content.
- A decision within 30 days of pilot end. No silent fade.

### 6.4 Pipeline math for the founder

To reach 250 qualified conversations in a year:

- **~50 LinkedIn connection requests/week** (personalized, ICP-filtered) → ~10–12 accepted → ~3–4 conversations.
- **~5 warm introductions/week** from alumni, diaspora, and customer referrals → ~2 conversations.
- **Community engagement** (10 groups, 15 min/day, useful answers, no links) → ~1–2 conversations.
- **Content inbound** after the case study → 1–2 conversations/week by Q3.

That is 6–8 conversations/week at peak, which fits the 5–6 target with room for no-shows. The bottleneck is follow-up discipline, not lead volume.

### 6.5 What "qualified conversation" means

A 15-minute call with an ICP-fit person who:

- describes the pain without being prompted ("we repeat the same instructions every morning"),
- names the current channel (WhatsApp voice notes, group messages, verbal briefing),
- knows who signs off on a $150–$600/month tool,
- and accepts or declines a 20-minute demo.

A polite "send me information" is not a qualified conversation. It goes to a low-touch nurture list and gets one follow-up with the case study.

## 8. Activation and onboarding

### 8.1 The activation definition

An organization is activated when the first instruction is shared within **24 hours** of signup (PRD §2, target ≥80% of orgs). For pilots, activation is engineered, not hoped for: the first instruction is created during kickoff.

The promise to keep (PRD TL;DR): **9 minutes from empty account to a shared instruction** for a first-time supervisor; **90 seconds** for a worker to finish the flow.

### 8.2 Concierge onboarding (pilots)

The founder does the setup. The supervisor's first experience is sending, not configuring.

| Step | Time | Owner | Artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kickoff call | 20 min | Founder + champion + buyer | Agenda, success criteria recap |
| Roster import | 10 min | Founder | CSV validated; language mix confirmed |
| Org settings | 2 min | Founder | Name, timezone, language |
| First instruction created live | 8 min | Champion (founder guides) | Voice note, 3 languages, preview confirmed |
| Worker page tested | 3 min | Champion + one worker | Real phone, real audio |
| Forward message copied | 2 min | Champion | EN/AR template from PRD Appendix |
| First share | — | Champion | Shared within 30 minutes of kickoff |

### 8.3 Self-serve onboarding (after v0.1.1)

For non-pilot customers, the product carries the onboarding:

- Setup checklist with persistent progress and the 9-minute promise (ACT-2).
- Demo crew (ACT-1): three sample workers, one instruction, full loop in 60 seconds.
- Inline empty states that explain the next action, not the feature.
- One onboarding email at Day 0 (magic link), Day 1 (first instruction), Day 3 (board), Day 7 (export), Day 14 (case-study/referral ask).
- A 60-second silent screen recording embedded on the setup page (no voice-over; works in an open office).

### 8.4 The first-week ritual (give the supervisor a script)

**Monday:** send the week's first instruction (voice, 30 seconds) before 8 a.m.
**Tuesday–Thursday:** check the board for unopened and help; forward the reminder copy to the group.
**Friday:** open the export once; note who needs a follow-up conversation.

This ritual is printed on a one-page card (EN/AR) and sent as a PDF after kickoff. It turns the product into a habit instead of a dashboard to visit.

### 8.5 Worker onboarding (no account, no training)

The supervisor's forward message is the onboarding. To make it work:

- The link opens on the name picker, not a login.
- The worker's language is remembered on the device; changing it takes two taps.
- Audio is the first control; text is always visible.
- The comprehension question has three large options; no typing.
- "I need help" is always reachable; it notifies the supervisor.
- A four-icon image ("tap link → pick your name → press play → answer") is provided to supervisors to forward with the link, language-neutral.

### 8.6 Diagnosing a slow first week

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix (within 48h) |
|---|---|---|
| <50% opened in 24h | Link buried in the group; sent after shift start | Re-send with the prefilled message at the start of the shift; pin the message |
| Opened but not answered | Name picker confusion; language mismatch; audio failed | Test the page on a worker's phone; confirm the language mix; re-record if audio is silent |
| High help rate (>25%) | Instruction too long or ambiguous; question tests trivia | Shorten to one action; regenerate the question; review the source transcript |
| Supervisor not sending | No ritual; unclear value; fear of mistakes | Monday/Friday ritual card; create the next instruction together on a call |
| Workers share the link outside the crew | Link forwarded beyond audience | Explain audience scoping; regenerate the link if needed (B3); no blame |

### 8.7 Activation metrics

| Metric | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first share (assisted pilot) | p50 ≤9 min, p90 ≤15 min | `instruction_shared.minutes_since_signup` |
| First instruction within 24h of signup | ≥80% of orgs (pilot: 100%) | PostHog |
| First worker completion within 24h of first share | ≥60% of targeted workers | Board / delivery events |
| Second instruction within 7 days | ≥90% of activated orgs | PostHog |
| Supervisor week-3 frequency | ≥3 instructions/week | PostHog |

## 9. Conversion: from pilot to paid

### 9.1 The value review (week 7, 30 minutes)

Attendees: economic buyer, champion, founder. No slides; one page of numbers.

**Agenda:**

1. Baseline vs. pilot (5 min): instructions sent, response, understood, help, time-to-share.
2. What changed for the supervisor (5 min): chase time, repeated explanations, who needed help.
3. What changed for the buyer (5 min): audit readiness, client questions, incident follow-up.
4. What still doesn't work (5 min): honest gaps, roadmap dates, no promises.
5. Three options and a price (10 min): continue at the pilot roster; expand to the full site; pause with a 90-day credit.

**Close:** sign the one-page order form or book a decision date within 7 days. Never leave with "we'll think about it" and no date.

### 9.2 The proposal (one page)

- Scope: site, workers, languages, start date.
- Price: per active worker, monthly or annual; pilot credit applied.
- What's included and what's not (the §4.3/§4.4 lists).
- Success metrics from the pilot and the quarterly review cadence.
- Payment terms and cancellation: monthly after the first quarter; 30 days' notice; data export on exit.
- Signature block; no auto-renewal clause in year one.

### 9.3 Procurement realities in the UAE

- **PO required?** Many SMEs pay by invoice without a PO; larger companies need vendor registration. Ask in the first conversation, not at signature.
- **Payment terms:** expect 30 days from larger customers; the first contract is quarterly prepaid to avoid financing their cash cycle.
- **Vendor forms:** keep a PDF pack ready — trade license (Lebanon), bank details, W-8BEN-E? (only if US-linked), insurance (not held yet), DPA, security summary. Do not overpromise certifications.
- **VAT:** customer may apply reverse charge; invoice states "VAT exclusive" and the Lebanese tax registration status. Confirm with an accountant.
- **Currency:** USD only. Do not quote AED and USD in the same document.
- **Collection:** invoice via Payoneer; follow up on day 7, day 14, day 21. A polite, firm cadence beats a discount. Pause service only after 30 days past due and two written notices.

### 9.4 Objection handling (full table in Appendix E)

The five that decide most deals:

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "WhatsApp is free." | "It is, and it stays your channel. We make the link in it count: each worker's language, one question, a record. You're not paying for messages; you're paying for proof." |
| "Workers won't use it." | "No install, no login, one tap. The pilot measures it: 70% response in 24 hours is the success criterion. If we can't hit it, you have evidence, not a bill." |
| "Translation will be wrong." | "That's why preview is mandatory and the supervisor confirms the source. We show every language side by side before anything is shared, and fall back to text if audio fails." |
| "We already have an employee app." | "Then run us beside it on one crew. Your app proves someone opened a message; we prove a Malayalam speaker answered the question correctly and pressed Done." |
| "It's too expensive." | "For 220 workers it's $374/month. One avoided rework incident or one hour a day of supervisor chasing costs more. The pilot fee is fully credited, and there's no per-seat charge." |

### 9.5 Conversion targets

- **50% of completed pilots convert within 30 days.**
- **70% of pilot customers are still active after six months.**
- **100% of converts have a named case study or a referral introduction** as part of the founding-customer terms.
- **No conversion discounts** beyond the design-partner 20% and the pilot credit. If price is the objection, reduce scope (fewer workers), not the rate.

### 9.6 If the pilot does not convert

Run an exit interview within seven days. Classify the reason:

| Reason code | Meaning | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Metrics hit, but the buyer doesn't believe it's worth the money | Share the ROI one-pager; offer a smaller scope; revisit in 90 days |
| Trust | Metrics hit, but data/security/company-size concerns block | Trust page, DPA, reference call with the design partner |
| Product | A missing capability (seats, WhatsApp API, integrations) | Log the v0.2 demand; do not build for one customer |
| Timing | Budget freeze, Ramadan, summer, restructuring | Nurture with the case study; check in at the next quarter |
| Fit | Wrong ICP, low usage, no champion | Disqualify; do not discount to keep a bad fit |

Every lost pilot gets a reason code in the CRM and a 90-day follow-up date. A "no" with a reason is an asset; silence is the enemy.

## 10. Retention and expansion

### 10.1 The health score

| Signal | Green | Yellow | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instructions/week | ≥3 | 1–2 for two weeks | 0 for 10 days |
| Response rate trend | Stable or up | Down 10 points | Down >15 points |
| Champion engagement | Opens weekly review | Misses one review | Misses two; no reply |
| Help resolution | <24h | 24–48h | >48h or unresolved |
| Board/export usage | Weekly export or board visit | Monthly | Never after week 2 |

**Red triggers a call, not an email.** The founder calls the champion, diagnoses, and logs the reason. Most churn in this segment is a lost habit, not a lost feature.

### 10.2 The retention ritual

Give each customer a simple operating rhythm:

- **Monday brief:** first instruction before 8 a.m.
- **Wednesday check:** board review; resolve help requests.
- **Friday proof:** export or board screenshot for the week; one follow-up conversation with a worker who missed.

The weekly digest (v0.1.1) automates the Friday proof. Until then, the founder sends a one-page Friday summary to the champion during the pilot; after conversion, the product's board is the summary.

### 10.3 Expansion inside the v0.1 product

v0.1 has one owner login and no seats (D5). Expansion in year one therefore comes from:

- **More workers on the same site** — pricing bands make this natural.
- **More crews** — same site, same supervisor, more languages.
- **Annual prepay** — cash and commitment, 2 months free.
- **A second site only when v0.2 seats ship.** Until then, a second site means a second owner login and a separate invoice; be honest about the limitation.

Do not sell multi-supervisor seats before they exist. Add interested customers to a seats waitlist and use the demand as the v0.2 business case.

### 10.4 Referrals

Ask twice:

1. **Pilot week 5**, if the metrics are green: "Who else in your network runs multilingual crews and complains about this?"
2. **After the first quarterly review**, with a specific ask: "Would you introduce me to the ops lead at {company}? I'll send a two-line note you can forward."

**Incentive:** one month free for the referrer and the referred customer, applied after the referred customer's first paid invoice. No cash, no MLM-style tiers.

**Target:** at least one qualified referral from every third customer in year one. The design partner's network is the single highest-yield source; protect that relationship.

### 10.5 Churn prevention in the first 90 days

| Risk | Trigger | Save play |
|---|---|---|
| Champion leaves or is reassigned | No login for 14 days; undeliverable email | Ask the buyer for a new champion; run a 20-minute re-onboarding |
| Roster changes (new crew, new languages) | Import not updated; response drops | Offer a roster refresh call; update language mix |
| Supervisor reverts to WhatsApp voice notes only | Instructions/week <1 | Rebuild the Monday ritual; create three instructions together |
| Price objection at renewal | Usage high but engagement low | Show the understood report; right-size the worker count; annual prepay |
| Product gap (seats, WhatsApp API) | Buyer asks for a capability we don't have | Log demand; offer a workaround for 90 days; do not promise a date |

## 11. Content and brand

### 11.1 Brand rules

- **Name:** Understood (pending the §3.7 decision) with «مفهوم» as the Arabic descriptor.
- **Voice:** plain, respectful, specific. Short sentences. No fear-mongering, no "AI magic," no "illiterate workers."
- **Visuals:** real sites, real phones, real hands. No stock handshakes, no generic dashboards, no fake diversity.
- **Languages:** show the six scripts accurately; never transliterate a language name; never use flags for languages.
- **Arabic:** written or natively reviewed; RTL layouts tested; no machine-translated slogans.
- **Worker dignity:** workers are the hero of the story, not the problem. The product exists so they understand, not so they can be monitored.

### 11.2 The narrative arc for year one

1. **The problem** (Nov–Dec): "Your WhatsApp group is not a briefing record."
2. **The demo** (Jan–Feb): "Watch a Hindi voice note become Malayalam audio in 20 seconds."
3. **The pilot evidence** (Mar–May): "220 workers. 74% response. 63% understood. One supervisor."
4. **The category** (Jun–Dec): "The record of who understood what."

Each phase has one primary asset and three supporting posts per week. Do not skip to the category before the evidence exists.

### 11.3 Content calendar — first 12 weeks (Appendix J)

Themes, formats, and channels are specified there. The operating rule: **batch on Friday, publish Mon/Wed/Fri, never miss a week.** Consistency beats polish at this stage.

### 11.4 The case study (the most important sales asset)

Structure (template in Appendix L):

- Customer profile: country, vertical, workers, languages, sites.
- Before: how briefings happened, chase time, audit answer.
- Pilot: duration, instructions sent, response, understood, help.
- After: supervisor quote, buyer quote, what changed.
- Limitations: what still doesn't work; no compliance claims.
- Consent: written, specific, revocable.

Publish in English and Arabic. Use an anonymized version until the customer approves the named version. Never include worker names, phone numbers, or faces without explicit consent.

### 11.5 Lead magnet: The 10-Minute Communication Audit

A one-page checklist for FM/HSE managers:

1. How many languages are in your crew today?
2. How does a new worker learn tomorrow's task?
3. How do you know a worker understood, not just received?
4. What do you show a client or auditor when they ask for proof?
5. How long does a supervisor spend chasing acknowledgements each day?
6. What happens when a worker says "I didn't understand"?
7. Which instructions are repeated most often?
8. What language does the safety briefing arrive in?
9. How do you update a worker's language when the roster changes?
10. What would one miscommunication cost this month?

Plus a roster language calculator: paste worker languages, see coverage and gaps. Delivered as a Google Sheet; no email gate for the checklist, email required for the calculator. No safety content, no compliance claims.

## 15. Legal, privacy, and trust

### 15.1 Data roles and posture

- The **customer is the data controller** for worker data; Understood is the **processor**. The pilot agreement includes a DPA-lite that states this.
- **Minimum PII:** worker name, language, crew, optional phone. Phone numbers are optional in the CSV; recommend customers leave them blank in v0.1 because the product never messages workers. No national IDs, no documents, no photos of workers.
- **Analytics:** pseudonymous org/user IDs only; no worker names, no phone numbers, no instruction content in PostHog (PRD §11).
- **Retention:** org data is kept while the account is active; deletion within 7 days of a confirmed request (SET-2). Nightly backups expire within 30 days; a deleted org is excluded from future restores by policy.
- **Worker notice:** the customer informs workers that instructions may be delivered through a link and that responses are recorded; the worker page footer carries the training disclaimer (WORK-10). Provide a one-paragraph notice template in the onboarding pack.

### 15.2 Regulatory scope

- **UAE PDPL** (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) applies to the pilot because workers are in the UAE. Be **PDPL-aware**, not "PDPL-certified": data map, lawful basis via the customer, DPA, deletion, breach process, no unnecessary PII.
- **Saudi PDPL** becomes relevant only when we sell in KSA; do not claim coverage before the KSA launch.
- **GDPR:** do not claim compliance. If an EU customer appears, run a proper review before signing.
- **Data residency:** do not claim UAE-hosted data until it is true. Publish the actual hosting region on the trust page before the first customer. If a customer requires in-country hosting, it is a 2027 decision, not a pilot promise.

When the pilot gate is met and ten customers are paying, the next chapter has a clear sequence:

## Appendix F — Pricing FAQ

1. **What is an active worker?** A worker targeted by at least one shared instruction in the billing month. Inactive workers are free.

7. **What if we stop mid-month?** Monthly plans: 30 days' notice, no refund for the current month. Annual: no refund, but the remaining value can be credited to another site within 12 months.

## Appendix L — Case study template

**Title:** "How {company} proved 220 workers understood the morning briefing — in four languages."

1. **Profile** — country, vertical, workers, sites, languages, current channel.
2. **Before** — how briefings happened; chase time per day; what they showed an auditor.
3. **Why they piloted** — the trigger (incident, client audit, new site, growth).
4. **The pilot** — duration, instructions sent, languages, response, understood, help.
5. **What changed** — supervisor quote, buyer quote, one concrete anecdote.
6. **What still doesn't work** — honest limitations (seats, WhatsApp API, glossary).
7. **The record** — a redacted CSV screenshot or a board view.
8. **Call to action** — "Run the same 8-week pilot."
9. **Consent** — written approval; named or anonymized; revocable; no worker PII.

Publish in English and Arabic. Repurpose into a carousel, a 60-second video, a sales email, and the pilot proposal's proof page.
